# Social studies differentiation pedagogy

Use with the shared R1–R8 rules in `../../../shared/core/pedagogy.md`.

## Preserve disciplinary thinking

All tiers retain the same essential question and target historical/civic/geographic/economic reasoning. Below cannot turn corroboration into “find one fact,” or argumentation into multiple choice.

## Common prerequisites

Two recurring access gates:

1. **content vocabulary** needed to understand sources;
2. **sourcing routine**: who produced the source, when, for whom, and why.

If sourcing is already secure, move the scaffold toward contextualization/corroboration.

## Supports

- SOAPS/HAPP-style annotation frame without prefilled conclusions;
- vocabulary bank;
- timeline/cause-effect organizer;
- map + structured questions;
- claim-evidence-reasoning frame;
- perspective matrix;
- source/evidence synthesis table.

Text/source excerpts can be annotated or chunked, while core evidence task stays intact. Avoid presentism, answer-embedded questions, fill-in-the-blank historical narratives, and single-cause simplification.

## Progression

Below supports should fade across tasks. Above extension adds a disciplinary operation such as corroborating more complex evidence, evaluating source reliability, constructing counterargument, historiographic tension, or civic proposal grounded in evidence.

## Flexible grouping

Use vocabulary/source-analysis/exit-ticket evidence to regroup. “Below” is a temporary instructional need for this lesson, not a fixed student identity.
